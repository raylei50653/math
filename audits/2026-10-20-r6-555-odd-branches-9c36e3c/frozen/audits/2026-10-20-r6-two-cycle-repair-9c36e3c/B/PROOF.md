## 2. The two cycles cut the sphere into the three actual components

Use the actual embedded dual. Each simple cycle C7 or O is a Jordan
curve. Their disjoint union has exactly three complementary regions.
Every primal P edge crosses its dual M edge and crosses no F edge;
thus a full P component lies in one complementary region of F.
Conversely, adjacency between primal vertices through a triangular
face region can cross only M dual edges. Equivalently, ordinary
embedded primal-dual component/face correspondence identifies these
three regions with the three full P components. Isolated primal
vertices would be retained in this correspondence; there are none
among the already identified components.

The region on the cap side of C7 contains exactly the centre K2.
The five matching spokes leave that cap toward O. The region between
the two Jordan curves contains U0, because it contains the complete
rim Q5 and all five rim-edge/spoke crossings. The remaining disk
bounded by O contains exactly T.

Consequently every matching chord of O is drawn in one of two
specified sides:

* A T edge dualizes to a chord in the disk on the side of O away
  from C7.
* A U0 edge off Q5 dualizes to a chord in the side of O containing
  C7.

In particular e and g are O chords on **opposite sides** of O:
e is on the T side, while g is on the cap side. This is an assertion
about their actual rotation and embedding, not merely an abstract
planar drawing chosen after the matching is fixed.

## 3. All cap-side chords are confined to literal interport intervals

The five spokes are pairwise disjoint arcs across the annulus between
C7 and O. They therefore occur in the same cyclic order on its two
boundaries, with the opposite boundary orientation convention if both
boundary walks are given the annulus orientation. They divide the
annulus into five disks. Each disk corresponds to one actual Q5
vertex and to the P tree attached to that vertex after deleting the
five Q5 edges.

Let h0 be any matching chord of O drawn on the cap side. Of the two
O intervals between its endpoints, exactly one bounds with h0 a disk
not containing C7. That interval contains no spoke endpoint. If it
contained such an endpoint, the actual spoke arc from there to C7
would have to cross h0 or cross the O interval, both impossible in
the given plane embedding. Therefore both endpoints of h0 lie
strictly inside one consecutive interport interval of O. All such
chords are noncrossing within that interval.

This proves the exact sector restriction. The chord g lies in the
sector of the actual rim vertex z: its primal edge mz is in the tree
attached to z. Neither a differently rooted U0 tree nor an arbitrary
cap-side chord may be renamed g.

## 5. An exact local size constraint on the g branch

Delete the primal bridge mz from U0 and let S_m be the component
containing m, with `b_m=|S_m|>=1`. It contains no Q5 vertex and none
of v,w,p,q,z. It is a tree attached to the remainder of U0 by the
single edge mz. Let A_g be the O interval which together with g
bounds the disk not containing C7, and let `ell_g=|E(A_g)|`.

The primal vertices in this disk are exactly S_m. All primal edges
leaving S_m are mz and the edges dual to A_g; no edge can leave this
Jordan disk elsewhere. Hence

`ell_g = sum_{x in S_m} d_J(x) - 2(b_m-1) - 1`

`       = sum_{x in S_m} d_J(x) - 2b_m + 1`

`       >= 3b_m + 3`.                                      (G1)

The last bound uses m's actual degree at least seven and degree at
least five at the other `b_m-1` ordinary vertices. This is a local
bound on a particular actual g interval, not a finite-search cutoff.

There are exactly `b_m-1` cap-side chords whose endpoints are in the
interior of A_g: they dualize to the tree edges of S_m. There are
no ports there. Counting the `ell_g-1` interior O vertices therefore
shows that precisely

`ell_g - 2b_m + 1 >= b_m + 4`                         (G2)

of them are endpoints of matching chords on the T side. One is the
dual vertex of the actual triangle mvw, hence an endpoint of e.
The second endpoint of e is not located by this count.

For comparison only, put `t=|T|` and `b=|U0|-5`. Counting all
vertices and faces gives

`|V(J)|=t+b+7`, `|O|=2(t+b)+3`.

The cap-m exclusion implies that every neighbour of m lies in T,
in `S_m-{m}`, or is z. Thus `t+b_m>=7`, and the weaker total bound
`|O|>=17` follows. This is **not a new completed-source size
exclusion**: the already frozen identity `|V(J)|=k-1` gives
`|O|=2k-13`, and the established fixed-r6 `k>=25` already gives
`|O|>=37`. The new usable information here is the local, named
interval statement (G1)--(G2).

## 6. A safe monotone chain is exactly a facial switch

Take either safe rim vertex r45 or r56, denoted rho; rho is distinct
from the original removed degree-six vertex r. Its only centre
neighbour is h. Its same-value neighbours are its two Q5 neighbours
and the children of the P tree attached at rho. All other neighbours
belong to T. This follows from the three full P components: an
additional same-value neighbour is necessarily in that attached
tree, while the complete centre stars supply no additional centre
incidence. In particular rho is an ordinary original vertex of
degree at least five.

Follow the boundary of the actual dual face corresponding to rho,
starting at the first spoke and proceeding on the O side. The next
dual vertex represents a triangle of precisely one of these forms:

* `(rho,child,a)` with `a in T`. Its M edge is the cap-side chord
  dual to `rho child`; following that chord stays on the same face
  boundary.
* `(rho,a,b)` with `a,b in T`. Its M edge is the T-side chord dual
  to ab. The face boundary itself has two consecutive O edges
  there, so an alternating walk restricted to the cap side stops.

After following a cap-side chord, the forward O edge in the same
interport interval is again the next edge of this face boundary.
This proves inductively that the monotone construction in
[A, section 3](../A/PROOF.md) follows exactly this face boundary
until it stops or reaches the other port. Its completion across
the two spokes and the short C7 edge is therefore the **entire
dual facial boundary of rho**.

Thus the exterior-side blocker has an exact primal meaning: two
consecutive external neighbours of rho are both in T. If no such
pair occurs, the cyclic incident edges at rho alternate between P
and its complement, and the facial boundary is M-alternating.
Let s be the number of attached tree children of rho. A successful
chain uses precisely those s child chords and forces

`d_J(rho)=2s+4`.                                         (S1)

The source degree lower bound excludes `s=0`, the adjacent-spokes
four-cycle switch, in either of these two safe sectors. A successful
source chain has `s>=1` and even degree at least six. This is a
source-level restriction on the conditional theorem, not a proof
that the conditional theorem triggers.

For one followed child chord, let S be its detached primal child
subtree, of order b. Every vertex of S is ordinary: it avoids all
six named vertices m,z,v,w,p,q, because m's branch is rooted at z,
the remaining marked vertices have their already fixed components,
and the safe root is distinct from z. If ell is its complete
skipped O-arc length, the same cut count as in section 5 gives

`ell = sum_{x in S} d_J(x) - 2b + 1 >= 3b+1`,

`ell+1 = sum_{x in S} d_J(x) - 2b + 2`.              (S2)

The complete new complement cycle made by this chord and its
skipped arc is even exactly when the sum of the original J degrees
in S is even, equivalently when S has an even number of odd-degree
vertices. In particular a single ordinary degree-five child creates
an actual complete odd five-cycle after this facial switch.
Its existence is compatible with the adopted original induced-C5
cap authority; that authority does not exclude a W5 boundary.

Combining (S1)--(S2) with A's full-cycle accounting gives the
following equivalent primal sufficient condition on either one of
these two actual rim vertices:

* its incident P and non-P edges alternate in the actual rotation;
* every attached child subtree has even degree sum.

When both hold, flipping the complete dual face gives an actual
even-complement matching in P101. If the joint even family is empty,
each of the two safe rim vertices must instead have either a
consecutive T-neighbour pair or a child subtree of odd degree sum.
These are actual source objects; they are not independent abstract
marginal obstructions. Their forced impossibility remains OPEN.

## 7. What the sector geometry does not prove

The cap-side interport graph consists of its O path and the
noncrossing matching chords lying in that sector. Internal vertices
whose matching edges lie on the T side are actual possible stops
for an alternating path restricted to this graph.

For example, conditionally suppose a particular rim vertex has no
attached U0 tree edges. Its interport interval has no cap-side
matching chord. If the O interval has at least two edges, there is
no alternating path within this sector from one port to the other:
after the first O edge the required M edge leaves the sector. This
is a conditional combinatorial obstruction, not an asserted source
example. The original degree lower bound does not logically rule
out the condition; a rim vertex can have several opposite-value T
neighbours.

Accordingly the preceding topology alone supplies neither the
existence of an alternating chain nor the parity of every complete
complement cycle after a flip. The frozen cyclic-cut exclusions
also cannot be invoked merely because a chain stops: one must
exhibit an actual forbidden cyclic cut, with both marks and the
correct full sides. No such general conversion is proved here.

The exact remaining source obligation is to obtain an actual
compatible alternating-cycle batch which avoids e, toggles f iff
g, and makes every complete complement cycle even; or to derive
an actual source contradiction when all such repairs fail. The
same-source two-cycle repair, joint positivity, omega_e=0 and
complete319 remain OPEN. Source realization is not triggered,
there is no new Lean theorem, and completed-source reconstruction
cost remains k+5.
