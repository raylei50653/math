## 2. A uniform two-used-colors obstruction

**U-USED-ONE.** Suppose U obeys the source-derived local facts: connected, only
one root owner, each U vertex original complete degree4, 2<=k<=3, bridge/odd-cycle
cactus, at least one private contact in each leaf block, at most one ordinary
private vertex in each odd-cycle block, adjacent-pair boundary-attachment cap,
and actual support at least three boundary points. For any proper boundary row
q using exactly three colors,

    |F_U(q) intersect q(B)| <= 1.

The proof is one common leaf-block argument, independent of arc orientation or
shape catalogue. Assume distinct used colors a,b both lie in F_U(q). Original
lists are

    L_t(v)=[4] \ (q(N_B(v)) union {t : rv is an original edge}), t=a,b.

Each is a degree assignment. Their uncolorability gives tightness and the
Gallai incident-block palette decomposition. In particular, at a private vertex
of a block its entire list is that block's palette.

First every odd cycle is a triangle, with no size restriction on U. For each
cycle cutpoint choose a private contact in a leaf block of an outside branch.
These contacts are mutually distinct and outside that cycle. Together with the
cycle's private contacts and at most one ordinary private vertex, the count
is |cycle|<=1+k<=4. This count is pin-independent; a cutpoint that is itself a
contact only strengthens it. An odd cycle therefore has length three.

Now consider a leaf block. A leaf bridge's private vertex has one original owner
edge and two actual adjacent boundary attachments, by degree4. Its two boundary
colors are distinct used colors. Tightness for both owner pins a and b would
require that boundary pair avoid both a and b. Only one used color remains,
so this is impossible.

A leaf triangle with one private contact and one ordinary private vertex also
cannot survive both pins. The ordinary vertex's list is a fixed two-color set,
while the contact's list is [4] minus its one actual boundary color and the owner
pin. Tightness requires the boundary color avoid both a,b. These two contact
lists differ when a is replaced by b, so both cannot equal the fixed ordinary
palette. Since at most one private vertex may be ordinary, each surviving leaf
triangle must have two private contacts.

A cactus with multiple blocks has at least two leaf blocks. The previous
paragraphs would require at least four distinct original owner contacts,
contradicting k<=3. Thus U has a single block. A singleton cannot have degree4
under the one-owner and boundary cap; a bridge is already excluded. Its cycle
is a triangle. Any ordinary private vertex yields the same changing-versus-fixed
palette contradiction. If all three vertices are contacts, their one actual
boundary attachment each must have the same q-color even for the first owner
pin a, since all three triangle lists are equal. A color class of a proper C5
has at most two vertices, so actual support would have at most two boundary
points, contradicting its size at least three. All cases of the block tree have
now been covered by the same leaf rule. This proves U-USED-ONE.

The theorem does **not** say that used colors are never forbidden. The negative
control in §5 shows a used forbidden color explicitly.
