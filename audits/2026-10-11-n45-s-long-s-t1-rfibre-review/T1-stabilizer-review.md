## 2. Two true rejections give the actual positive odd bridge path

Let root colours a,d be distinct true forbidden colours of the same actual two-contact piece, keeping all other external colours fixed. For L/S the unique extra s-contact has the same fixed colour b in both queries. For U the variable root is s and there is no second fixed root. At each original piece vertex v, deletion of external neighbours leaves a list of size at least deg_T(v), because each actual external incidence removes at most one colour from Col and the complete degree is4. If external colours repeat, the inequality becomes strict. Lemma7 therefore makes all lists tight in each rejected query; in particular both queries have no such repeated external colours at a vertex.

The only lists which change between the a/d rejection certificates are those at the two distinct root-contact vertices u,v. Their signed colour differences are nonzero, with a and d interchanged. All other vertex lists, including a fixed s-contact if it lies off the root contacts, are identical. Theorem10 provides block palettes for each rejected query. Palettes of blocks incident at a vertex are disjoint and their union is its whole tight list.

In the actual block-cut route from u to v, consider all off-route rooted branches. Every non-root vertex in such a branch has the same list in the two certificates. Starting with remote leaf blocks, a non-root vertex's list minus already fixed descendant palettes determines that block's palette uniquely. Thus every off-route palette agrees between the two certificates. This induction retains all branch vertices/blocks and works at arbitrary depth.

The nonzero palette difference forced at u must propagate along the block route: at an internal route vertex the unchanged full list and unchanged off-route palettes force the two incident route-block differences to cancel. If a route block were an odd cycle, it would have a third vertex distinct from the two route vertices. That third vertex and all of its off-route branches have unchanged lists/palettes, so they force the cycle palette difference to be zero. This contradicts the nonzero difference propagating from u. The same argument covers u and v lying in the same odd-cycle block. K4 blocks are already excluded in the source structure; hence every route block is an actual bridge, not an edge arbitrarily selected from a cycle.

At the u endpoint, after its unchanged off-path palettes are removed, the bridge palettes are {d} for root=a and {a} for root=d. At each internal path vertex these singleton palettes interchange. The v endpoint requires the original ordering {d}/{a} again, so the original path length is odd. It is positive because the two root contacts are distinct. Every edge of this path is an actual bridge, and its removal separates the complete original pieces W_j used by T1.

For L/S, the fixed extra s-contact can be on the path, inside an off-path branch, or identical to one of the original r contacts. Its constraint is the same unary list subtraction in the two true-rejection queries. If it shares a root-contact vertex, true-rejection tightness already prevents the fixed b from coinciding with either queried forbidden root colour. It creates neither a new independent coordinate nor an extra palette difference.

## 3. Residual pair and the full-W stabilizer

For W_j rooted at path vertex x_j, let D_j be the actual boundary colours at x_j together with b when x_j is the fixed s-contact, and Q_j the union of all incident off-path block palettes. All palettes making up Q_j are common to the a/d certificates. For an internal path vertex the two remaining bridge palettes are {a} and {d}, so the pre-root-pin residual is F={a,d}. At an endpoint, write E_j=Col−(D_j∪Q_j). Its two bridge residuals satisfy E_j−{a}={d} and E_j−{d}={a}; these two exact equalities give E_j=F. Therefore every endpoint and internal point satisfies D_j∪Q_j=Col−F.

Now let pi fix every actual boundary colour occurring anywhere in the complete W_j, and also fix b if the actual s-contact lies anywhere in W_j. All non-root lists in every off-path branch are preserved. Permuting its existing palette certificate gives another certificate with those same non-root lists; rooted uniqueness makes its palettes equal to the originals. Hence pi Q_j=Q_j. The root's actual fixed-colour set D_j is also preserved. Taking complements gives pi F=F.

The whole actual W_j support is essential: inspecting only x_j's boundary attachments would not justify invariance of deeper palettes. The argument does not assert that palette sets are proper colourings or replace all original assignment preimages by available-colour marginals. At q1 for U, the two certificates are obtained anew from the assumed true s=1 and s=3 rejections; beta's palettes are not renamed as q1 palettes.

## 4. U Gallai/K4-free application and local edge release

At the original beta, the inherited F_U={2,3} supplies a true rejection of fixed s=2 on connected actual U. Every U vertex retains full degree4, and its only exterior neighbours lie in B∪{s}. Tightness/Gallai therefore gives the actual U block decomposition. To apply the connected-exterior K4 lemma, local colourability after every U-incident edge deletion must also be established. It follows without a special whole-X deletion witness at s=2:

- Deleting a B attachment or an s-contact edge removes one genuinely distinct forbidden external colour at its U endpoint, since true-rejection tightness made all external colours there distinct. U remains connected and this endpoint has strict list surplus, so the full U region colours.
- Deleting an internal nonbridge edge leaves U connected, with unchanged lists and reduced endpoint degrees. There is strict surplus.
- Deleting an internal bridge leaves two connected components, each with a deleted-edge endpoint whose original list now has strict surplus. Both components colour completely with their original fixed exterior constraints.

All cases produce full local assignments of the entire U−edge region under that fixed exterior colouring. This is a local degree-list consequence, not a claim that all other pieces have a compatible global lift at s=2. It is exactly the local fact required by the frozen K4 proof.

Suppose J is a K4 block. Each original J vertex has its three clique neighbours and exactly one exterior direction. If that direction remains inside U, it is a bridge into an attached component; an additional odd-cycle direction would already contribute two incidences. Different such attached components cannot meet or reconnect two J vertices, since that would contradict J being an actual block. Deleting that bridge yields nonempty endpoint-colour sets on both sides. Original rejection means no unequal pair can be joined; therefore both sets are the same singleton. An attached side having no B/s neighbour would permit arbitrary palette permutation of its proper colourings and could not have a singleton endpoint-colour set. Thus that side actually meets B∪{s}.

Choose the first such exterior contact in each direction. The four resulting original tethers have disjoint internal vertices. B∪{s} is connected in X through the retained original s-spoke b0 or b2. Merge that exterior and the tether interiors as one hub, retaining the four J vertices as singleton bags: the six original clique edges and four tether adjacencies give K5. The omitted rb4 is unused. Planarity therefore excludes K4. Larger clique blocks would already contain K5 as a subgraph; remaining Gallai blocks are bridges/odd cycles. No exterior degree4 assumption is made about the hub, which may contain boundary vertices and s.
